# What Is an Oil Shock?

James D. Hamilton (2003), *Journal of Econometrics* 113(2), 363–398

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4076(02)00207-5) · [free version (NBER)](https://doi.org/10.3386/w7755)

## The question

- What is the right way to measure an oil shock, if output does not respond to oil prices in a simple linear way?

## How he answers it

- A flexible nonlinear regression of US GDP growth on oil price changes, plus an instrument approach using exogenous disruptions in petroleum supply.

## What he finds

- Clear nonlinearity: price increases matter much more than decreases, and increases that only undo earlier falls matter less.
- This is the idea behind the "net oil price increase" measure. Oil shocks matter because they disrupt spending by consumers and firms on key sectors.

## What we use from it

- Further motivation for testing asymmetry (H2), and the reason we look at rises and falls separately in the NARDL.
