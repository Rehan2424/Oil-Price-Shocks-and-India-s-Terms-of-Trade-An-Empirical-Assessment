# Oil and the Macroeconomy When Prices Go Up and Down: An Extension of Hamilton's Results

Knut Anton Mork (1989), *Journal of Political Economy* 97(3), 740–744

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1086/261625)

## The question

- The oil price collapse of 1986 did not bring a boom. Do price rises and price falls have the same effect?

## How he answers it

- Extends Hamilton's US regressions through the 1986 price collapse, with separate terms for oil price increases and decreases.

## What he finds

- Price increases lower US output growth significantly. Price decreases do not raise it by a similar amount: the effect is asymmetric.

## What we use from it

- The origin of our H2 (rises and falls have unequal effects), which we test with a nonlinear ARDL.
- Our answer when H2 fails: Mork's asymmetry is about output, while the terms of trade is a price ratio that passes oil prices through in both directions.
