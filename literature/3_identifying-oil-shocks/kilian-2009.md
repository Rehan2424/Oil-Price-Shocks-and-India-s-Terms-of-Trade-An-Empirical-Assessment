# Not All Oil Price Shocks Are Alike: Disentangling Demand and Supply Shocks in the Crude Oil Market

Lutz Kilian (2009), *American Economic Review* 99(3), 1053–1069

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.99.3.1053) · [free version (SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=975262)

## The question

- Do oil price shocks have the same effects whatever caused them?

## How he answers it

- A structural VAR of global oil production, global real economic activity and the real oil price, separating three shocks: oil supply, global demand for all industrial commodities, and oil-specific demand.

## What he finds

- Each shock has different effects on the oil price and on the US economy, which is why simple regressions on oil prices are unstable.
- The oil price surge of the 2000s was driven mainly by global demand, which helps explain why it did not cause a major US recession.

## What we use from it

- The idea behind H3: the cause of an oil price rise may matter for India's terms of trade.
