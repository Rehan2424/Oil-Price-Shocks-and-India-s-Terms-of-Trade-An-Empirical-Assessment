# Oil and the Macroeconomy since World War II

James D. Hamilton (1983), *Journal of Political Economy* 91(2), 228–248

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1086/261140)

## The question

- Is the link between oil price increases and US recessions real or a coincidence?

## How he answers it

- US data for 1948–72, with Granger-causality tests of oil prices on output and checks that the correlation is not spurious.

## What he finds

- All but one US recession since World War II was preceded by a sharp rise in the price of crude, typically about three quarters of a year earlier.
- The correlation is statistically significant and not spurious, so oil shocks were a contributing factor.

## What we use from it

- Why oil shocks are treated as a major macro shock in the first place, and the starting point for the asymmetry debate behind H2.
