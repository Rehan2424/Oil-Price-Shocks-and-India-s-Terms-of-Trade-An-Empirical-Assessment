# Modelling Asymmetric Cointegration and Dynamic Multipliers in a Nonlinear ARDL Framework

Yongcheol Shin, Byungchul Yu and Matthew Greenwood-Nimmo (2014), in *Festschrift in Honor of Peter Schmidt*, Springer, 281–314

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4899-8008-3_9)

## The problem it solves

- How to let a variable's rises and falls have different short-run and long-run effects inside a cointegration model.

## The method

- Split the regressor into two partial sums, one of all its increases and one of all its decreases, and put both in an ARDL (the NARDL). It is estimated by OLS, and the bounds test still applies.
- Dynamic multipliers trace the path from the short run to the long run after a rise and after a fall. The paper illustrates it with the unemployment–output relationship in the US, Canada and Japan.

## What we use from it

- Our H2 test: separate long-run and short-run effects of oil price rises and falls, with Wald tests of symmetry.
- Result: short-run symmetry is never rejected (p = 0.54–0.91 across seven specifications), so H2 is not supported.
