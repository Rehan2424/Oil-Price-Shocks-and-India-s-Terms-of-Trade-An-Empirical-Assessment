# Trade Liberalisation and "Revealed" Comparative Advantage

Bela Balassa (1965), *The Manchester School* 33(2), 99–123

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9957.1965.tb00050.x)

## The idea

- Comparative advantage cannot be observed directly, but trade flows reveal it.
- A country's revealed comparative advantage (RCA) in a product compares the product's share in its exports with its share in world exports. Above 1 means an advantage.

## What it was built for

- Balassa used export performance to study which industries would gain from trade liberalisation among industrial countries.

## What we use from it

- The RCA index taught in lecture 8, applied to India's refined petroleum (SITC 334): 0.06 in 1999, 1.26 in 2000, 4.37 in 2025 (UNCTADstat).
- This acquired comparative advantage in refining is why India's exposure to oil prices has roughly halved.
