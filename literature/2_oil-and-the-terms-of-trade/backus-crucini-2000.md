# Oil Prices and the Terms of Trade

David K. Backus and Mario J. Crucini (2000), *Journal of International Economics* 50(1), 185–213

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-1996(98)00064-6) · [free version (NBER)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w6697)

## The question

- Why are the terms of trade so volatile, and why do their correlations with output and trade volumes keep changing?

## How they answer it

- A dynamic general-equilibrium trade model with an oil-producing region, compared with data for industrial countries.

## What they find

- Oil accounts for much of the variation in the terms of trade over the previous 25 years, and its role varies a lot over time.
- The economy responds differently to oil supply shocks than to other shocks, which explains the unstable correlations.

## What we use from it

- Our anchor paper. We ask its question, how much oil moves the terms of trade, for India, an emerging importer that also exports refined fuels.
- Its point that oil supply shocks behave differently from other shocks leads to our H3.
