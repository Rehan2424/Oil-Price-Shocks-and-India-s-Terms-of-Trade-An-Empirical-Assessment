# The Terms of Trade, the Real Exchange Rate, and Economic Fluctuations

Enrique G. Mendoza (1995), *International Economic Review* 36(1), 101–137

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.2307/2527429)

## The question

- How much of the business cycle in open economies comes from terms-of-trade shocks?

## How he answers it

- A three-sector intertemporal equilibrium model (exportables, importables, non-tradables), calibrated and compared with a large multi-country database.

## What he finds

- Terms-of-trade shocks account for nearly half of GDP variability in the model.
- The model also reproduces the weak correlation between net exports and the terms of trade, and large deviations from purchasing power parity.

## What we use from it

- One side of the debate on our literature slide: in models, terms-of-trade shocks are a big deal.
- Together with Schmitt-Grohé and Uribe (2018), it is why we measure the oil effect for India instead of assuming its size.
