# Currency Depreciation, Income, and the Balance of Trade

Arnold C. Harberger (1950), *Journal of Political Economy* 58(1), 47–60

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1086/256897)

## The question

- What does a change in the exchange rate, and the change in the terms of trade that comes with it, do to a country's trade balance once its effect on income is taken into account?

## How he answers it

- A Keynesian open-economy model in which import prices feed into real income, and real income into saving and spending.

## What he finds

- A worse terms of trade lowers real income. People protect their consumption by saving less, so spending falls by less than income and the trade balance worsens.
- Together with Laursen and Metzler (1950), this is the Harberger–Laursen–Metzler (HLM) effect.

## What we use from it

- The reason our question matters: a fall in India's terms of trade is a real-income loss that spills into the current account, as in 1990-91, 2013 and 2022.
- The HLM effect is our answer to "so what?" in the viva (question bank B24).
